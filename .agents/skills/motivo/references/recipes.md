# Seven editorial recipes

| Recipe | Story construction | Asset evidence | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Faceless essay | Specific question → evidence → explanation → earned conclusion | Sources, images/clips, narration | Stock montage disconnected from words |
| Ranked discovery | Selection criterion → distinct entries → synthesis | Source for each entry, original commentary | Arbitrary ranking and repeated cards |
| Product detail | Real need → visible detail → demonstrated benefit → qualified CTA | Actual product photos/specifications | Invented personal experience or fake before/after |
| Product comparison | User need → fair criteria → tradeoffs → who each suits | Comparable, dated specifications | Unverified prices or guaranteed outcomes |
| Visual explainer | Concrete question → mechanism → worked example → takeaway | Accurate diagrams, units, evidence | Decorative charts and fake data |
| Place & culture | Sensory opening → geographic/historical context → human detail → resolution | Verified facts, licensed visuals/audio | Generic exoticism and false footage attribution |
| Short drama & sketch | Setup → conflict → emotional turn → payoff | Supplied footage, illustrated assets or authorized generated clips; consistent character/voice notes | Identity drift, unnatural exposition and unsynchronized replacement dialogue |

For drama, read [drama.md](drama.md) for production routes, character continuity, coverage and speech. The wider user-facing use-case catalog is `docs/VIDEO_TYPES.md` at the project root; several use cases may share a narrative recipe.

Choose duration from narrative density. Thirty seconds does not always need four equal scenes. For lists, vary framing and tempo while preserving comparison clarity. Original research and narration are valuable; a template alone does not establish originality or monetization eligibility.

Create the shotlist before code. Each row needs seconds/frames, intent, asset path, visible action, sound cue, and caption. Sum frame counts; subtract transition overlaps. Keep the final shot alive for its full duration. For spoken content, time captions from the actual recording, not a guessed reading speed. Do not compress host speech to force a preset runtime.
