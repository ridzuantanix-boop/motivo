# Art direction, not decoration

Choose a visual thesis that belongs to the subject. A story about metal can use precision, brushed surfaces, aligned rules, hard crops, and mechanical timing; a personal essay can use scanned textures, marginal notes, pauses, and restrained dissolves. Do not apply the same look to both.

Three shipped explorations:

- **Pressroom:** bone paper, ink, vermilion, monumental serif, asymmetric editorial columns. Reveal type through a horizontal mask; use sustained holds. Best for essays/history.
- **Signal:** acid chartreuse, charcoal, oversized grotesk, directional strips, precise cuts. Animate geometry to explain a process instead of sprinkling floating shapes. Best for explainers/launches.
- **Atelier:** oxblood, parchment, restrained serif, generous silence, an isolated object and measured details. Best for product stories using actual product assets.

These are starting points. A style choice must change composition, type hierarchy, shape language, motion, and pacing, not merely recolor a template.

## Motion grammar

Give each sequence one dominant action. Distinguish entrance, reading hold, and exit. Cut when the idea changes; do not transition just because time elapsed. Build anticipation through a still hold, then contrast it with decisive movement. Use easing that fits weight; avoid spring overshoot on every text element. Let meaningful match cuts, masks, tracking, negative space, and scale carry the edit. Avoid ornamental parallax on a flat photo.

Typography is a visual asset. Work with line breaks, baseline, width, optical alignment and deliberate font contrast. No automatic card around every sentence. No default purple-blue gradient, glass dashboard, particle field, emoji iconography, endless counters, or karaoke bounce unless the brief has a concrete reason for it.

Audio is a separate pass: purposeful room tone, cuts, music phrasing and selective impact sounds. No whoosh on every cut. Duck music around speech and check intelligibility on small speakers. License or create every sound; do not silently fetch tracks.

Use safe margins appropriate to the platform and keep captions separate from headline hierarchy. Test portrait independently. Do not invent a universal safe area that guarantees every platform's current UI.
