# Short drama and sketches

Use for fictional scenes, comedy sketches and serialized stories. Read the brief before prescribing genre or duration. Ask for the premise/genre if absent, then offer two brief ideas rather than a long questionnaire. Propose two characters, one location and 30–60 seconds for an easy first production, clearly as defaults rather than limits.

## Choose an achievable production route

- **Supplied live action:** plan/edit actual supplied recordings. Do not pretend the agent filmed missing coverage.
- **Illustrated/photo drama:** compose stills with controlled movement, narration or separate dialogue audio. No lip-sync claim; a voiceover is not evidence that a character visibly speaks.
- **Generated live action:** use the chosen provider after checking current capabilities and authorized budget. Read `realism.md` and provider guidance. Do not promise consistent actors, perfect realism or one-request complete films. Break complex exchanges into controlled shots when the provider cannot reliably handle them.

## Direct the scene before code

Write a logline and emotional turn, then a compact script with speaker, actual dialogue, visible action and sound. Give each character an objective and a distinct speaking pattern. Make dialogue conversational; remove explanatory monologues and slogan-like lines unless the character calls for them. Let a reaction or silence do work. Structure setup → conflict → turn → payoff; an open ending is valid when briefed.

Create `projects/<slug>/characters.md`: stable character IDs, authorized visual references, appearance, modest wardrobe for BM production, voice source/voice assignment, speaking pace and relationships. Keep the same assignment per character. Record props, location layout, time/light, entry/exit, eyelines and screen direction in the continuity notes. Carry these forward for episodes.

Plan coverage with purpose: establishing/context shot, action/dialogue and useful reactions. Keep the camera axis and eyelines coherent unless a deliberate motivated change is designed. Avoid random shot-size changes or a transition effect after every line. Do not cut before an emotional response can read. Provider clip-length limits are verified constraints, not a reason to force every dramatic beat to equal length.

## Speech and sound

For generated on-camera speech, supply actual dialogue in the provider prompt where supported, preserve the original generated audio and playback speed, and inspect the lip-sync. If it fails, repair/replace the shot or switch to a clearly voiceover/illustrated approach. Do not place new Google TTS over a visibly speaking face and claim synchronization.

Google TTS, recordings and manual AI Studio exports can supply narration or separate character lines for illustrated drama. The bundled Google client is single-speaker per request: separate line files and consistent voice assignments are supported workflows, not an implemented automatic multi-speaker pipeline. If a different multi-speaker tool is proposed, verify its capabilities first. Never infer permission to replicate someone's voice from a photo reference.

Balance dialogue, ambience and selective effects. Fit timing to the actual recordings. Captions must identify speakers when otherwise ambiguous, and must not reveal a twist before it is heard. Check emotional rhythm in playback, not only through stills.

Before delivery, inspect character/prop/voice continuity, each speaking mouth, shot joins, audio gaps, captions and payoff. Use `quality-gate.md`. Record what was actually watched/listened to; live generation and cross-episode identity are not guaranteed. Fictional reenactments must not be presented as real documentary evidence.
