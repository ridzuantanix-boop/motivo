# Live-action realism gate

Photorealism is a target, never a guarantee. Prefer genuine supplied footage when documentary truth or real product behavior matters. Label illustrative generated scenes in the project notes; do not pass them off as evidence of an event or product test.

## Plan for controllability

Specify a plausible location, time, motivated light source, lens/framing, one primary action, camera support/movement, wardrobe, and audio. Use physical descriptions instead of a stack of “8K cinematic masterpiece” adjectives. Avoid incompatible lens/depth/light cues. Provide actual product and identity references when authorized. Keep identity, wardrobe, geometry, weather, shadows, and props consistent in a continuity ledger.

Use stable shots and short actions when hands, lettering, crowds, reflections, eating or object manipulation are involved. Add readable typography in post rather than trusting generated signage. Multi-shot provider prompts are optional: prefer separate controlled shots when continuity fails. Do not use film grain or rapid cuts to conceal a known defect.

## Inspect temporal behavior

Extract contact sheets for every clip, then inspect suspicious intervals frame-by-frame and watch the clip. Check hands, teeth, eyes, ear/jewelry continuity, skin detail, edge shimmer, object permanence, reflections, shadows, foot contact, inertial movement, cloth, background people, lip-sync, and audio artifacts. A good still does not establish good motion. Preserve original speech audio and playback speed.

For Malay productions retain the supplied modest-dress rules, including background people. For narration retain natural voice guidance; use provided audio without requiring an API.

Reject obvious morphing, extra limbs, product/logo mutation, identity drift, impossible mechanics, slipping feet, robotic speech and broken lip-sync. Re-generate only the affected short interval if authorized, inspect the join, or substitute a real asset/simpler shot. Record attempts and cost; do not spend beyond the agreed cap. Never report “100% real.”
